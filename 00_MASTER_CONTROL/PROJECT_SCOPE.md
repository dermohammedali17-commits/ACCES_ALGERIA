# ACCESS ALGERIA
# PROJECT SCOPE

> Defining what Access Algeria does, what it does not do, and where its strategic role begins and ends.

---

# 1. PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT

This document defines the operational and strategic scope of Access Algeria.

Its purpose is to ensure that the platform remains focused on its core mission and does not expand into activities that are outside its strategic capabilities, legal position or business model.

Access Algeria is primarily a B2B Market Entry and Business Development platform.

Selective engagement with public institutions and government ecosystem stakeholders may be relevant when a project requires legitimate institutional understanding, regulatory coordination or formal stakeholder engagement.

---

# 2. PRIMARY OPERATING MODEL

Access Algeria operates primarily as a:

B2B Market Entry Platform

The primary focus is on supporting business relationships between:

- International companies
- Global brands
- Manufacturers
- Investors
- Algerian companies
- Strategic commercial partners

The core purpose is to help international organizations better understand and approach the Algerian market.

# 4. SELECTIVE B2G AND INSTITUTIONAL ENGAGEMENT

Access Algeria may interact with relevant public-sector and institutional stakeholders when such engagement is directly relevant to a market-entry project.

This may include:

- Understanding institutional ecosystems
- Mapping relevant public stakeholders
- Identifying regulatory pathways
- Supporting formal stakeholder preparation
- Coordinating legitimate institutional engagement where appropriate

Potential stakeholder categories may include:

- Investment promotion institutions
- Relevant ministries
- Regulatory authorities
- Public agencies
- Sector-specific institutions
- Economic development bodies

Access Algeria does not replace official legal, regulatory or governmental processes.

---

# 5. OUT OF SCOPE ACTIVITIES

Unless specifically authorized, legally structured and supported by appropriate partners, Access Algeria is not intended to operate as:

- Vehicle importer
- Automotive dealer
- Product distributor
- Product retailer
- Manufacturer
- Customs broker
- Law firm
- Financial institution
- Licensed investment advisor
- Government authority
- Regulatory authority

Access Algeria should remain focused on its role as a strategic market-entry and business-development platform.

# 7. PROJECT ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

A potential project should generally meet one or more of the following conditions:

- Clear connection to the Algerian market
- Strategic relevance to Access Algeria
- Potential commercial value
- Need for market intelligence
- Need for market-entry strategy
- Potential for strategic partnerships
- Ability to create reusable knowledge

Projects should be assessed before significant resources are committed.

# 9. FLAGSHIP PROJECT PRINCIPLE

Access Algeria should use selected real-world projects to validate and improve its methodology.

A flagship project should:

- Test the Access Algeria methodology
- Generate practical market intelligence
- Develop strategic capabilities
- Create reusable knowledge
- Produce documented lessons

The Aston Martin Algeria case represents an important reference project for the development of the Access Algeria methodology.

---

# 10. SCOPE DECISION TEST

Before accepting or pursuing a new activity, Access Algeria should ask:

1. Is this activity consistent with our Market Entry mission?
2. Does it create strategic value for the client?
3. Is it legally and operationally appropriate?
4. Do we have the necessary capability?
5. Does it require an external expert or partner?
6. Does it create reusable knowledge or capability?

If the answer to these questions is unclear, the scope should be reassessed.

---

# 11. WHEN EXTERNAL EXPERTS ARE REQUIRED

Access Algeria may require qualified external support for:

- Legal advice
- Regulatory interpretation
- Tax matters
- Accounting
- Financial structuring
- Technical assessments
- Engineering
- Specialized sector expertise

Access Algeria should coordinate expertise when necessary but should not present external specialist work as internal expertise.
